Every group name has the same shape: the central atom, a dash, then the ligands
in brackets with a count where there is more than one.

So `C-(C)2(H)2` is a carbon bonded to two carbons and two hydrogens, which is
the methylene of an alkane chain. `CO-(C)2` is a carbonyl carbon bonded to two
carbons. A ketone.

## The ligands are not counted twice

A ligand says what the central atom is bonded to. It does not contribute that
atom to the molecule.

Take `C-(C)3(H)`. The three bracketed carbons are each counted by their own
group, and counting them here as well would give every carbon in the molecule
one extra for each neighbour it happens to have.

Hydrogen is the exception, and at present the only one. A hydrogen never has a
group of its own, so the group it is written in is the only place it is ever
counted.
