Click a group to add it. Click its count to take one back.

The running total stays on screen in kJ/mol and kcal/mol, headed by what it is
a total *of*. On a phone it sits at the foot of the page; swipe it up, or press
the button beside it, to see the working out.

## Finding a group

You do not have to type Benson notation. Search by the shorthand you would
write instead: `CH3` finds `C-(C)(H)3`, and `methylene` finds `C-(C)2(H)2`.
Common names work too. `methyl`, `ketone` and `hydroxyl` all resolve to the
group they name, and the list of them lives in `notation/synonyms.csv`.

This works because the calculator reads the notation rather than searching the
text of it. A carbon carrying three hydrogen ligands is findable as `CH3` even
though those three characters appear nowhere in `C-(C)(H)3`.

You can narrow to one category or to several at once. Several is normal; most
molecules need groups from two or three of them.

The table view shows every value beside its unit, quantity and source.

## Keyboard

- `/` jumps to the search box
- arrow keys move through the increments
- `Enter` adds the one in focus
- `+` and `-` adjust its count

## Getting the working out

`Copy` puts the whole calculation on the clipboard as plain text, laid out as a
column so it stays lined up when pasted into a report. `Undo` takes back the
last addition. `Reset` clears everything.
